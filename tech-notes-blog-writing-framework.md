# Tech Notes Blog Writing Framework

## Purpose

Use this framework as the default writing process for every Tech Notes blog post.

The goal is to create useful, reader-focused, original, well-structured articles that are easy to understand, genuinely valuable, visually engaging, and optimized for search without sounding like SEO copy.

This framework is inspired by the 10-step ProBlogger workflow supplied by the site owner. It should guide the writing process, but the resulting articles must be original and must not copy ProBlogger's wording or structure unnecessarily.

---

## Step 1: Pick a Strong Topic

Before writing, identify the core reader need.

Prefer topics based on one or more of these:

- A question the reader wants answered
- A problem the reader wants solved
- A task the reader wants to complete
- A goal the reader wants to achieve
- A concept the reader wants explained clearly
- A current technology, engineering, AI, software, or science development worth understanding

Define the topic in one sentence.

Ask:

> What specific value will this article give the reader?

Avoid broad topics unless they can be given a clear, useful angle.

---

## Step 2: Think About the Reader

Define the intended reader before writing.

Identify:

- Who they are
- Their likely knowledge level
- What they already know
- What they may misunderstand
- What problem or question brought them to the article
- What they are likely worried or curious about
- What they should be able to understand or do after reading

Write a short internal reader statement:

> "This article is for [reader] who wants to [goal] but needs help understanding [problem]."

Write with empathy and clarity.

Do not assume expert knowledge unless the topic specifically targets experts.

---

## Step 3: Create a Working Headline

Create a working title before drafting.

The headline should communicate:

- The main topic
- The reader benefit or interesting angle
- What makes this article worth opening

Prefer specific headlines over generic ones.

Good headline patterns include:

- How to...
- Why...
- What...
- X Explained
- X vs Y
- A Practical Guide to...
- Everything You Need to Know About...
- What Happens When...
- The Real Reason...

Do not use clickbait that the article cannot deliver.

After writing the article, review and improve the headline.

---

## Step 4: Brainstorm and Outline

Create the article's "bones" before writing the full prose.

List the major questions the article must answer.

Typical outline:

1. Hook / introduction
2. Context and definition
3. Core explanation
4. Main practical or technical sections
5. Examples, comparisons, or evidence
6. Common mistakes / misconceptions where relevant
7. Practical implications
8. FAQ where useful
9. Conclusion
10. One clear call to action

Remove weak or repetitive sections.

Arrange sections in a logical progression.

Each section should naturally lead to the next.

---

## Step 5: Critically Review the Outline

Before drafting, challenge the outline.

Ask:

- Is this genuinely useful?
- Does it answer the reader's actual question?
- Is there a clear reason to read the entire article?
- Is there a strong "aha" or "fist-pump" moment?
- Is anything unnecessary?
- Are important questions missing?
- Do I need more research?
- Are any claims likely to be outdated?
- Is the order logical?
- Can the reader finish the article with a clearer understanding than when they started?

For technical, scientific, financial, medical, legal, or current topics, verify important facts with authoritative sources before making factual claims.

---

## Step 6: Write a Strong Introduction

The introduction should usually be 1–3 short paragraphs.

It should:

1. Establish the problem, question, surprise, or opportunity.
2. Show that the article understands the reader's situation.
3. Explain why the topic matters.
4. Tell the reader what they will gain by continuing.

Avoid generic openings such as:

- "In today's rapidly changing world..."
- "Technology is evolving faster than ever..."
- "Have you ever wondered..."

Start with something specific.

The first paragraph should create a reason to continue reading.

---

## Step 7: Expand the Main Points

Write the body using the outline.

Rules:

- One main idea per section.
- Use descriptive H2 headings.
- Use H3 headings for useful subsections.
- Keep paragraphs reasonably short.
- Explain jargon when first introduced.
- Use examples when they improve understanding.
- Prefer concrete explanations over abstract claims.
- Use tables for genuine comparisons.
- Use bullet lists when they improve scanning.
- Avoid filler.
- Avoid repeating the same point in multiple sections.
- Maintain a natural, human conversational tone.
- Use technical terminology where appropriate, but explain it clearly.

For tutorials, give actionable steps.

For technical explanations, explain both "what" and "why."

For comparison articles, define the decision criteria before comparing.

For news/current topics, distinguish confirmed facts from interpretation.

---

## Step 8: Write the Conclusion and One CTA

The conclusion should close the loop opened by the introduction.

It should:

- Reinforce the main lesson
- Summarize the most important points
- Answer the original question
- Explain what the reader should take away

Then provide ONE clear call to action.

Examples:

- Try the technique described in the article.
- Check the official documentation.
- Explore the linked source.
- Test the code.
- Read the related article.
- Leave a useful comment or question.

Do not overload the ending with multiple competing CTAs.

The CTA must make sense for the article and the Tech Notes goal.

---

## Step 9: Add Depth and Visual Appeal

After the first complete draft, improve it.

Look for opportunities to add:

- A useful image
- Diagram
- Chart
- Comparison table
- Code example
- Real-world example
- Timeline
- Formula
- Technical illustration
- Relevant video
- Expert quotation
- Primary-source reference
- Related article

Only add visuals when they genuinely improve understanding.

Do not add decorative elements just to make the article longer.

For scientific or technical topics, prioritize diagrams, comparisons, and explanatory visuals.

---

## Step 10: Edit and Proofread

Perform a separate editing pass after drafting.

Check:

### Accuracy
- Facts are correct.
- Dates are correct.
- Numbers are verified.
- Technical terminology is accurate.
- Current information has been checked.
- Sources support important claims.

### Structure
- Headline matches the article.
- Introduction leads naturally into the body.
- Sections are in logical order.
- No major reader question is left unanswered.
- Conclusion closes the loop.

### Writing
- Remove unnecessary words.
- Remove repetition.
- Fix grammar and spelling.
- Improve awkward sentences.
- Keep the voice natural.
- Avoid excessive jargon.
- Avoid generic AI-sounding phrases.
- Prefer active voice where appropriate.

### Reader experience
- Paragraphs are readable on mobile.
- Headings make the article easy to scan.
- Lists and tables are used appropriately.
- Important information is easy to find.
- The article provides real value rather than simply summarizing a topic.

# Tech Notes SEO Requirements

Every article should also receive an SEO pass.

## SEO Title

Create a clear, compelling title that naturally includes the primary topic.

Avoid keyword stuffing.

## Meta Description

Write a concise description that:

- Explains what the article covers
- Includes the primary topic naturally
- Gives the reader a reason to click

## URL / Slug

Use a short, descriptive, lowercase slug.

Example:

`roman-space-telescope.html`

Avoid unnecessary words and dates unless they are important to the topic.

## Headings

Use:

- One H1
- Logical H2 sections
- H3 subsections where useful

Do not use headings only for visual styling.

## Search Intent

Before publishing, identify the likely search intent:

- Informational
- Navigational
- Commercial investigation
- Transactional
- Tutorial / how-to

Make sure the article satisfies the intent rather than simply targeting keywords.

## Internal Links

Where relevant, link to other Tech Notes articles.

Do not create artificial internal links where none are useful.

## External Sources

For factual, scientific, technical, or current information, prefer authoritative primary sources.

Examples:

- NASA
- Official government agencies
- Official documentation
- Research institutions
- Standards organizations
- Original project repositories
- Official company documentation

Avoid relying on low-quality aggregators when primary sources are available.

## Structured Data

Where appropriate, include valid Schema.org structured data such as:

- Article
- FAQPage
- HowTo

Only use structured data that accurately represents visible page content.

## Analytics

Preserve the site's existing Google Analytics implementation when editing existing pages.

Do not remove existing tracking code unless specifically instructed.

---

# Tech Notes Voice

Write like a knowledgeable person explaining something to an interested reader.

The tone should be:

- Clear
- Practical
- Conversational
- Confident
- Helpful
- Precise
- Human

Avoid:

- Corporate buzzwords
- Excessive hype
- Empty introductions
- Artificially dramatic language
- Repetitive summaries
- Keyword stuffing
- Unnecessary disclaimers
- Overly academic prose when a simple explanation works

Use technical language when it improves precision, but explain specialized terms.

---

# Originality Rule

Use the framework as a writing methodology, not as text to reproduce.

Never copy sentences, paragraphs, examples, or distinctive phrasing from the source material.

Research independently and write original explanations.

When using information from external sources, summarize it in original language and link to the relevant authoritative source when appropriate.

---

# Final Article Quality Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- [ ] Topic has a clear reader benefit
- [ ] Target reader is clear
- [ ] Working headline was created and reviewed
- [ ] Outline is logical
- [ ] Article answers the main reader question
- [ ] Introduction has a strong hook
- [ ] Main sections are useful and non-repetitive
- [ ] Technical terms are explained
- [ ] Examples or evidence are included where useful
- [ ] Visual opportunities were considered
- [ ] Conclusion summarizes the key lesson
- [ ] Exactly one primary CTA is used
- [ ] Facts and current claims are verified
- [ ] SEO title is optimized
- [ ] Meta description is written
- [ ] Slug is clean
- [ ] H1/H2/H3 hierarchy is correct
- [ ] Internal links were considered
- [ ] Authoritative external sources are included where needed
- [ ] Structured data is valid and relevant
- [ ] Existing analytics code is preserved
- [ ] Grammar and spelling are checked
- [ ] Article reads naturally on mobile
- [ ] No unnecessary filler remains
- [ ] Article provides a meaningful reason for the reader to finish

---

# Default Instruction for Future Blog Requests

When asked to write a Tech Notes blog, silently follow this framework from Step 1 through Step 10.

Do not output the internal workflow unless the user asks for it.

The final article should feel like a finished publication, not a writing exercise.

For current topics, research and verify facts before writing.

For existing Tech Notes articles, preserve the site's existing SEO, analytics, navigation, styling, and URL structure unless the user explicitly asks to change them.
